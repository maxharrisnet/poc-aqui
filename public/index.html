  • Hover the coloured bar to see what each part of that total costs, or open a row for the full breakdown and a link to the listing.
  • Press Watch to keep a record. Set a peso limit and it tells you when a copy drops under it.

Eight steps, about twenty seconds.

This demo does a small version of what the full system would do continuously in the background. Here's the whole chain, start to finish, so nothing about the numbers is a black box.

  1. You give it a list

    Here you type it. In the real system this is your Discogs want list, synced automatically, plus the artists and labels you follow — so you never type anything at all.

  2. It finds the release on Discogs

    Using the official Discogs API and nothing else. No scraping, ever — Discogs' terms prohibit it, and your account holds your inventory, so an enforcement action would hit the shop and not just the tool.

  3. It reads the current lowest price

    Live marketplace figures: what the cheapest listed copy costs right now, and how many copies are for sale. Both come straight from Discogs at the moment you press the button.

  4. It converts to pesos

    At today's European Central Bank reference rate. The real system also stores the Banxico FIX rate, which is what SAT expects when the purchase is later reconciled for accounting.

  5. It adds shipping

    Estimated from the pressing country. The full system replaces these estimates with real carrier quotes, then keeps sharpening them against what shipments actually cost once they arrive.

  6. It applies Mexican customs

    Records from the US and Canada under about USD $50 come in free under USMCA. Everything else picks up the roughly 19% tasa global, and then 16% IVA on top of value plus duty.

  7. It flags anything needing a broker

    Above roughly USD $250 a shipment needs formal customs entry through a licensed broker. That's a delay and a fee, not just a bigger number, so it gets called out rather than buried in a total.

  8. You get one number, and a link to buy

    A single peso figure per record, sorted cheapest first, with the cost broken into its parts and a direct link to the Discogs marketplace. You decide; nothing is ever bought automatically.

What's real here, and what isn't

Worth being precise about, because two of these numbers are solid and two are placeholders standing in for work that belongs in the real build.

Real, live data

  • Discogs prices and stock — pulled live from the official API each time you press the button.
  • Release metadata — artist, label, catalogue number, format, year, cover art, all from Discogs.
  • Exchange rates — today's ECB reference rate, fetched at the start of every run.
  • Customs arithmetic — the de minimis, tasa global, IVA and formal-entry logic all genuinely run.

Placeholder, pending real work

  • Shipping costs — sensible estimates by country, not live carrier quotes. Every one is labelled low confidence.
  • The customs figures themselves — from public reporting, not yet confirmed by a licensed broker.
  • Shipping origin — we use the pressing country, because the official API won't tell us where the seller actually is.
  • Matching — a search takes Discogs' best guess. Reissues and repressings aren't reconciled against each other.

Read before trusting these numbers

Five things that would change a total, and that this demo cannot yet get right.

  • Customs rules are not broker-validated. Duty, IVA and the formal-entry threshold come from public reporting on Mexico's 2026 Customs Law, not from a licensed broker.
  • Origin is the pressing country, not the seller's location. Customs depends on where a parcel actually ships from, and the official Discogs API doesn't expose that. A German pressing may well be sold from Guadalajara.
  • Shipping is illustrative, not a live carrier quote. The real system refines these from actual purchase data over time.
  • This is one snapshot, not continuous monitoring, and it takes the single cheapest copy listed — not the best copy at a fair price.
  • No identity resolution yet — reissues, repressings and regional variants aren't matched against each other.

The whole system 🗺️

Four sources feed one matching problem, which feeds one cost engine, which writes to two sheets and comes out three ways. The demo you're looking at is the solid path through the middle.

✅ Built & running 🔨 Known how, not built ❓ Still unproven

The one genuinely uncertain box is identity resolution — deciding that an eBay listing, a Discogs pressing and a Bandcamp announcement are the same record. Barcodes make it tractable where they exist. Where they don't, it's free-text matching, and free-text matching is never finished, only tuned.

What the APIs won't give us 🚧

Each of these is a hard ceiling, not a to-do. They shape what the product can honestly promise, so they're worth knowing before the roadmap is set.

📀 Discogs

  • No marketplace listings. The API publishes a lowest price and a stock count, and nothing per-copy. Condition, seller and seller location simply aren't available.
  • So origin is a guess. We use where the record was pressed, because where it ships from isn't offered. A German pressing may sit in Guadalajara.
  • 60 requests a minute. Every record costs two, so a sweep is paced rather than parallel.
  • Scraping is out. Their terms prohibit it, and the account at risk is the shop's.

🛒 eBay

  • No release identifiers. Listings are free text, so joining them to a Discogs pressing is a matching problem, not a lookup.
  • Roughly 5,000 calls a day on the standard tier — generous, but it caps how often a large watchlist can be swept.
  • Shipping to Mexico must be asked for explicitly, per request, or the quote comes back for the wrong country.

📧 Bandcamp

  • No API at all for searching what's for sale. The developer API serves artists reporting on their own sales.
  • No prices in the emails. None. So a Bandcamp find can never carry a landed cost — only a link and a date.
  • We can't follow a label programmatically, or even ask whether we already do. Ian follows; the app learns the roster from the mail.
  • Coverage is the follow list — it answers "what's new", never "who has this record".

🎧 Spotify

  • No vinyl. It's a streaming catalogue: no format, no pressing, no stock, no price.
  • The merch isn't in the API. Artist-page merch runs through a third-party storefront integration with no public endpoint, so we can't read it even though you can see it.
  • Barcodes are usually the digital release, which often differs from the vinyl pressing's. A strong hint, not an identity.
  • Recommendations and audio features were withdrawn for new apps in late 2024. Nothing should be built on them.
